Evaluating a live copilot to align AI design with the agency of the humans in the loop

Research · a mixed-methods case study with Noora Health and the Stanford Center for Health Education

The setting

Noora Health's Mobile Care Companion answers families' health questions over WhatsApp. Since November 2025, a GenAI copilot reads incoming queries and drafts language-matched replies for Medical Support Executives (MSEs), who review, edit, and send every message. The copilot never faces patients: its effects run through MSE judgment.

240kqueries per month across India
80kusers reached monthly
15khealth facilities
6languages, Hindi to Odia

The study · concurrent mixed methods

Copilot access9 MSEs randomized 1:1,
by language No copilot9 MSEs
RQ1

How do MSEs experience the copilot: attitudes, trust, agency, everyday practice? 18 interviews across both arms, plus every edit to an AI draft read as a behavioral trace of where the tool meets or falls short of MSEs' needs.

RQ2

Do conversations measurably differ? Expert rubrics for clarity, medical safety, empathy, and clear next steps, scaled with a validated LLM judge across the full corpus in English and Indic languages.

Then

Findings triangulated: multilevel models compare the two groups, interpreted alongside what MSEs say in interviews.

The point of the evaluation is to align copilot design with MSEs' agency: findings feed the next iteration, so the tool strengthens frontline judgment rather than replacing it.
