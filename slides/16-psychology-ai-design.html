Alignment

The core argument

AI should be understood as an extension of human purposiveness, grounded in practical autonomy: the human capacity to act for reasons and take responsibility. Alignment then runs through a normative interface connecting machine behavior to human norms, with humans remaining the ultimate bearers of responsibility.

arXiv preprint · 2026

Psychological competence is a missing dimension in AI evaluation.

Marcos Economides et al., 2026

Flip to see details
Evaluation

The core argument

Benchmarks reward accuracy, reasoning, and compliance, but say little about whether a system supports user cognition, emotional interpretation, and behavioral decision-making in ways fit for the user and context. The authors propose testing this with scenario-based probes and structured human and model-assisted evaluation.

Nature · 2019

AI needs a behavioral science of machine and human-machine behavior.

Rahwan, Cebrian, Obradovich et al., 2019

Flip to see details
Field

The core argument

AI systems increasingly mediate social, cultural, economic, and political interaction. Understanding their effects requires an interdisciplinary science of machine behaviour that combines computer science with behavioral, social, and cognitive science methods.

We also see a unique opportunity in bringing the science of wise psychological interventions into AI design.

We are working on practical guidelines and papers for agency-supportive AI using wise intervention techniques.

Education assistants that protect belonging by helping users interpret struggle as a normal part of learning, rather than evidence that "people like me don't belong here."
Health or livelihoods coaches that strengthen users' sense of control by helping them recognize meaningful choices and act on options available to them.
AI messaging tools that deliberately test how small changes in language shape identity, social norms, and motivation - for example, framing an action as part of "who I am," not just something to do.
