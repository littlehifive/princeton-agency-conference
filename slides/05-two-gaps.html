Working with these organizations surfaced two gaps related to human agency for AI products in the social sector.

The Design Gap

AI products are built to optimize service delivery, rather than explicitly to strengthen people’s agency.

Despite AI's potential to strengthen human agency, NGO products often treat agency as a byproduct rather than an explicit design objective.

Decades of research in psychological science and psychologically wise interventions identify agency as a key mechanism for improving development outcomes, yet it is rarely intentionally built into AI design, testing, or iteration.

The Measurement Gap

AI products are underused as windows into how human agency evolves.

Many products may not generate as many signals of agency as tools like ChatGPT or Claude Code do. Even when these signals exist, they often come from power users, and the field lacks a shared standard or analytical tools for measuring and interpreting agency inside human-AI digital traces. Recent advances in LLM as a measurement tool (e.g., OpenAI's Gabriel and Google's Langextract offer promising solutions beyond traditional NLP methods. )
